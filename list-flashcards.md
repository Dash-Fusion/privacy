# Flashcards — Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** 2026-09-04
**Last updated:** 2026-09-04

## Summary

Flashcards keeps your decks on your phone.

**Nothing you write ever leaves your device.** There is no account, no sign-up,
no cloud, and no server of ours anywhere. Your decks are not uploaded, not
analysed, and not used to train anything. We cannot read them and we could not
hand them to anyone if we were asked, because we never receive them.

That is worth stating plainly, because a deck is often more personal than it
looks: the vocabulary you are struggling with, the exam you are preparing for,
the names and terms from a job you have not started yet. All of it stays in this
app's own storage.

The only data that leaves your device is what Google AdMob needs to show ads,
and **no card of yours is ever part of that**.

## What the app stores, and where

Everything is written to this app's **private storage**, which no other app on
your phone can read. The following is kept there and never leaves your device
unless you export it yourself:

- **Your decks** — their names, and when each was created and last changed
- **Your cards** — the front and back text of every card
- **Each card's review schedule** — when it is next due, its current interval,
  its ease factor, and how many times it has been reviewed or lapsed
- **App preferences:** theme, Material You colour, and when the rewarded
  ad-free window runs out

We hold **no copy of any of it**. There is no analytics SDK in this app, no
crash reporter of our own, and no telemetry. None of your text is transmitted
anywhere, and none of it is used for advertising.

The app opts out of Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer, so your
decks are not copied into Google's backup service either.

**Consequence, stated honestly:** because the decks exist only on your phone, we
cannot recover them if you lose the device, and uninstalling the app deletes
every deck permanently. Use the CSV export for anything you cannot afford to
lose.

## Import and export are things you do, not things we do

The app can export a deck to a `.csv` file and import cards from one. Both go
through Android's own file picker (the Storage Access Framework): **you** choose
the file to read or the place to write, Android grants the app access to that
one file, and nothing passes through us.

Because of that the app requests **no storage permission at all**. It cannot see
your other files, and it cannot write anywhere you have not pointed it at.

An export is a plain text file of your own card text. Where you put it — a
folder on the device, a cloud drive, an email to yourself — is your choice, and
once it is there it is governed by whatever you sent it to, not by this policy.

## Third-party services we use

### Google AdMob (ads)

The app is supported by ads served by Google AdMob. Ads are the only reason the
app needs an internet connection at all; making decks, importing, exporting,
searching and studying all work fully offline.

The app shows **no banner ads, no native ads and no ads on launch**, and — the
part that matters most in an app like this — **it never shows an ad during a
study session.** A full-screen ad between a card and its answer would break the
one moment the app exists for, so the study screen cannot show one at all. What
the app shows is an occasional full-screen ad *after* a session has been
finished and you are back at your decks, and an optional rewarded ad you can
choose to watch to switch ads off for 24 hours.

To serve ads, Google receives data from your device, which may include your
device's advertising identifier (`AD_ID`), coarse location inferred from your IP
address, device and app information, and ad-interaction data. **It does not
receive your decks, your cards, your search terms or your review history** — the
app never sends those anywhere. Google's processing is done as an independent
controller under its own policies:

- Google Privacy Policy: <https://policies.google.com/privacy>
- How Google uses information from partner sites and apps:
  <https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites>

### Consent (UMP)

Where required by law, the app shows Google's User Messaging Platform consent
form before ads are initialised, and records your choice. In the EEA and the UK
you are asked about personalised advertising; in covered US states you are
offered the opt-out required there. You can change your decision at any time
from **Settings → Ad privacy options**, and if you decline, every study feature
continues to work exactly as before.

## Data we collect ourselves

**None.** We operate no servers, no accounts and no analytics. We have no
database with your name in it and no way to identify you or your device. We do
not sell or share personal information, because we do not have any.

## Permissions the app requests

- **Internet / network state** — solely so the Google Mobile Ads SDK can fetch
  ads. Turning off Wi-Fi and mobile data means no ads load; every other feature
  still works.
- **Advertising ID (`AD_ID`)** — required by Google Play for apps that serve
  ads; used by AdMob as described above.

That is the entire list. The app requests **no storage permission, no camera, no
microphone, no contacts, no location, and no notifications**, and it runs no
background or foreground service — it does nothing at all while you are not
looking at it.

## Children

This app is not directed at children and is intended for users aged 13 and over.
It ships no bundled decks and no child-directed content. We do not knowingly
collect personal information from children — as described above, we hold no
personal data at all.

## Your choices

- **Ad personalisation:** change your consent decision any time via
  **Settings → Ad privacy options**.
- **Reset your advertising ID or opt out of personalised ads** in Android's own
  settings, under **Settings → Google → Ads**.
- **Take your data with you** at any time using the CSV export. It is your text,
  in a format any other app can read.
- **Delete your data** by deleting decks in the app, or by clearing the app's
  storage or uninstalling it. Because the decks live only on your device, that
  removes them completely and irreversibly — there is no copy anywhere else, and
  no deletion request to send us.
